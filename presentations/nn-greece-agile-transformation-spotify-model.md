## Slide 2 — A word before we start

> "Agile transformation is not about adopting a framework.
> It is about changing how the organisation thinks, decides, and delivers."

Most transformations stall because they install the **mechanics** of agile
(stand-ups, sprints, boards) without changing the underlying **operating model**.

Today we focus on the operating model first.

## Slide 3 — What is an Agile Transformation?

| Traditional Organisation | Agile Organisation |
|---|---|
| Deliver to a long-term plan | Deliver and adapt continuously |
| Decisions escalate upward | Decisions made closest to the work |
| Teams organised by function (IT, Claims, Finance…) | Teams organised around customer outcomes |
| Annual budgeting cycles | Continuous value-based funding |
| Change is a risk to manage | Change is an expected input |

**Transformation = shifting the entire operating model**, not just IT.

## Slide 4 — Why Now for NN Greece?

**The insurance industry is under pressure from three directions:**

1. **Customer expectations** — digital-native competitors (insurtech) set the bar for speed and experience. Customers compare NN to Amazon, not to other insurers.

2. **Regulatory velocity** — DORA, IDD, IFRS 17 and future EU directives require faster adaptation cycles.

3. **Group alignment** — NN Group is already advancing its own agile maturity. NN Greece can leverage group learning rather than start from zero.

**At 600 people, NN Greece is the ideal size to transform meaningfully without the complexity paralysis of a 10,000-person organisation.**

## Slide 5 — Why the Spotify Model?

There are several agile scaling frameworks (SAFe, LeSS, Nexus, DA). We are focusing on the **Spotify Model** today because:

- It was **designed for product-oriented, cross-functional teams** — not IT delivery projects
- It **balances autonomy and alignment** without heavy ceremony overhead
- It is **principle-based, not prescription-heavy** — it adapts to your context rather than forcing you into a rigid process
- It has been adopted (and evolved) by financial services organisations globally — ING Bank Netherlands being the closest parallel to NN Greece's context

> ING Bank Netherlands ran a Spotify-inspired transformation starting in 2015, reorganising ~3,500 staff. Their story is directly relevant to NN Group entities.

## Slide 7 — The Squad

**The Squad is the primary unit of delivery.**

- **Size:** 6–10 people (Dunbar's "two-pizza rule")
- **Composition:** fully cross-functional — product, tech, data, design, operations, compliance (where relevant)
- **Ownership:** one customer journey or product area, end to end
- **Autonomy:** the squad chooses *how* to work (Scrum, Kanban, hybrid)
- **Accountability:** one **Product Owner** sets priorities; the team owns delivery

**Key principle:** a squad should be able to release value to customers **without waiting for another team's approval or capacity.**

### Example squads for NN Greece

| Squad | Owns |
|---|---|
| Motor Claims Squad | End-to-end motor claims journey |
| New Business Squad | Quote-to-policy digital flow |
| Customer 360 Squad | Unified customer data & self-service portal |
| Broker Connect Squad | Broker portal and integrations |
| Core Platform Squad | Shared infrastructure & APIs (internal customer) |

## Slide 8 — The Tribe

**The Tribe is a cluster of squads working on related customer journeys.**

- **Size:** typically 40–150 people (so that the Tribe Lead can know everyone)
- **Led by** a **Tribe Lead** — a servant leader, not a delivery manager
- **Governed by** a **Tribe Council** (all Squad POs + Chapter Leads) that aligns priorities without micromanaging squads
- **Budget** is allocated at tribe level, not per project

### Example tribes for NN Greece (600 people)

| Tribe | Focus | Approx. Squads |
|---|---|---|
| Personal Lines Tribe | Motor, Home, Travel products | 4–5 squads |
| Claims Experience Tribe | All claims journeys across products | 3–4 squads |
| Digital & Data Tribe | Digital channels, analytics, AI/ML | 3–4 squads |
| Operations & Compliance Tribe | Policy admin, regulatory, finance ops | 3–4 squads |

> At 600 people, NN Greece would have approximately **3–4 tribes** — a very manageable scope.

## Slide 9 — The Chapter

**The Chapter solves the "who develops my people?" problem.**

When everyone is in a cross-functional squad, the traditional line manager disappears. The Chapter replaces that function.

- A Chapter groups **people with the same skill** across all squads within a tribe (e.g., all software engineers in the Digital Tribe, all actuaries in the Personal Lines Tribe)
- Led by a **Chapter Lead** who is *also* a working member of one squad (not a full-time manager)
- The Chapter Lead handles: **career development, hiring, craft standards, skill-building**
- The squad handles: **day-to-day work assignments and priorities**

**This is one of the most important innovations of the Spotify model:** it separates *people development* from *work management*.

### Example chapters for NN Greece

- Engineering Chapter (per tribe)
- Data & Analytics Chapter
- Product Management Chapter
- Claims Operations Chapter
- Compliance & Risk Chapter

## Slide 10 — The Guild

**The Guild is an organic, cross-tribe community of practice.**

- Completely **voluntary** — anyone across the entire organisation can join
- No budget, no formal authority — driven by passion and shared interest
- Focused on **spreading knowledge, avoiding reinventing the wheel**, and building culture
- Meets regularly (monthly chapter-of-practice sessions, Slack channels, demos)

### Example guilds for NN Greece

- Agile Practices Guild (coaches, Scrum Masters, chapter leads)
- Security & Privacy Guild
- Customer Experience Guild
- AI & Automation Guild
- Accessibility & Inclusive Design Guild
- Greek Insurance Regulation Guild (unique to the local context)

> Guilds are how NN Greece stays connected to NN Group best practices without creating formal bureaucracy.

## Slide 11 — Autonomy with Alignment: The Key Tension

The most common question: **"If squads are autonomous, how does anything stay aligned?"**

Spotify's answer: **"Be loosely coupled, but tightly aligned."**

```
                HIGH ALIGNMENT

                      │
         Micromanaged │  High-performing
         (no trust,   │  (the goal)
          slow)       │
                      │
LOW ──────────────────┼────────────────── HIGH
AUTONOMY              │                  AUTONOMY
                      │
         Chaos        │  "Spinning wheels"
         (no          │  (well-intentioned but
          direction)  │   misaligned effort)
                      │
                LOW ALIGNMENT
```

**Tools for alignment without micromanagement:**

| Tool | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Company OKRs (Objectives & Key Results) | Set the "why" and "where" for everyone |
| Tribe OKRs | Translate company direction to tribe context |
| Squad OKRs | Squad's own contribution — squad chooses the "how" |
| Tribe Council syncs | Weekly/bi-weekly cross-squad dependency check |
| Demo days | Visibility into what every squad is shipping |
| Architecture principles | Guardrails, not gates |

## Slide 12 — What Changes for Leadership?

This is the most important slide in the deck.

**The Spotify model does not just reorganise teams. It fundamentally changes what leaders do.**

| Old leadership behaviour | New leadership behaviour |
|---|---|
| Assign work to teams | Set clear outcomes; let teams find the path |
| Approve decisions | Define decision boundaries; remove obstacles |
| Protect your department's territory | Share resources and expertise across tribes |
| Manage by activity (timesheets, hours) | Manage by outcomes (OKRs, customer metrics) |
| Escalation culture | "Disagree and commit" culture |
| Annual performance review | Continuous coaching (Chapter Lead model) |

> **The hardest part of the Spotify model is not the structure — it is the leadership culture shift.**
> Tribes and squads fail when leaders revert to command-and-control under pressure.

## Slide 13 — What About Middle Management?

A common concern: **"What happens to my managers?"**

In the Spotify model, the managerial functions are redistributed, not eliminated:

| Traditional Manager Role | Who does it in Spotify |
|---|---|
| Sets priorities and backlog | Product Owner |
| Develops team members | Chapter Lead |
| Removes blockers | Agile Coach / Scrum Master |
| Strategic direction | Tribe Lead |
| Architecture decisions | Tech Lead (within squad) |
| Budget accountability | Tribe Lead + Finance |

**Strong middle managers often become excellent Tribe Leads, Chapter Leads, or Product Owners.** The transformation is an opportunity to move great people into roles where they have more impact — not to eliminate them.

## Slide 15 — The Transformation Journey (3 Horizons)

### Horizon 1 — Prepare & Pilot (Months 1–6)
- Leadership alignment workshops — agree the "why" and the model
- OKR design for company and first tribe
- Select a **pilot tribe** (recommendation: Digital & Data — lower regulatory risk, high visibility)
- Train Product Owners, Chapter Leads, and Scrum Masters
- Run first 2 sprints with coaching support

**Outcome:** One tribe operating at basic squad level. Lessons learned documented.

---

### Horizon 2 — Scale & Stabilise (Months 7–18)
- Roll out model to remaining tribes
- Establish Tribe Councils and cross-tribe dependency management
- Launch first Guilds (Agile, Security, CX)
- Integrate NN Group agile community (leverage group CoP)
- Reform HR processes: continuous feedback, Chapter Lead career paths
- Adjust budgeting process from project-based to tribe-based

**Outcome:** Full organisation operating in the new model. Early OKR cycles complete.

---

### Horizon 3 — Optimise & Lead (Month 18+)
- Measure: delivery speed, customer satisfaction (NPS), employee engagement
- Share NN Greece story with NN Group as a case study
- Evolve the model — no framework is static; squads adapt their practices
- Extend to non-IT functions: HR squads, Finance squads, Claims operations fully integrated

**Outcome:** Agile is the operating system, not a programme.

## Slide 16 — Where to Start: The First 90 Days

**You do not need to transform everything at once. You need a credible first move.**

### Recommended 90-day sprint

```
Week 1–2:   Leadership offsite — align on vision, agree pilot tribe, appoint sponsors
Week 3–4:   Map current state — which teams exist, what do they own, who are the leads?
Week 5–6:   Design pilot tribe structure — squads, POs, Chapter Leads
Week 7–8:   OKR workshop — company OKRs → pilot tribe OKRs → squad OKRs
Week 9–10:  Agile Fundamentals training for all pilot tribe members
Week 11–12: First sprint kicks off. Coaches embedded. First retrospective.
```

**Single most important decision:** Who are the **Product Owners**? They are the engine of the model. Identify and develop them first.

## Slide 17 — Risks and How to Mitigate Them

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Leadership reverts to command-and-control under pressure | High | Executive coaching, OKR accountability, regular leadership retros |
| "Agile" becomes a rename of the same waterfall work | High | Focus on outcomes (OKRs), not activities (velocity metrics) |
| HR processes don't support the new model | Medium | Early HR involvement; Chapter Lead role formally recognised in job architecture |
| Compliance and risk teams feel excluded | Medium | Embed compliance skills inside squads; create a Compliance & Risk Chapter |
| NN Group mandates conflict with local squad autonomy | Medium | Agree "group non-negotiables" upfront; everything else is squad choice |
| Pilot tribe is chosen poorly (too much regulatory sensitivity) | Low–Medium | Start with Digital & Data or a greenfield product area |

## Slide 18 — What Success Looks Like (12 Months)

**Quantitative signals:**
- Time-to-market for a new product feature: from 6–9 months → 4–6 weeks
- Number of releases per quarter: measurable increase
- Employee engagement score in pilot tribe: +10–15 points vs. baseline
- Customer NPS on digital journeys: measurable improvement

**Qualitative signals:**
- Leaders spend more time removing obstacles than attending status meetings
- "We can't do that, it's another department's problem" is said less and less
- Teams talk about customer outcomes, not internal deliverables
- NN Group uses NN Greece as a reference for local-entity agile transformation

## Slide 19 — Key Takeaways

1. **Agile transformation is an operating model change**, not a tooling or process change.

2. **The Spotify model** gives NN Greece a proven, principle-based blueprint that balances team autonomy with strategic alignment.

3. **At 600 people**, NN Greece is the ideal size — large enough to benefit meaningfully, small enough to move quickly.

4. **The four building blocks** — Squad, Tribe, Chapter, Guild — each serve a distinct, non-overlapping purpose.

5. **Leadership behaviour change** is the single biggest success factor and the single biggest risk.

6. **Start small, learn fast.** A well-run pilot tribe in 90 days teaches more than 12 months of planning.

7. **NN Group is an asset**, not a constraint — group agile communities, ING learnings, and group OKRs can accelerate the NN Greece journey.

## Appendix B — Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Squad** | Small (6–10), fully cross-functional, autonomous team owning one product area |
| **Tribe** | Cluster of related squads (40–150 people) sharing a customer domain |
| **Chapter** | Horizontal grouping of same-discipline people within a tribe; led by a Chapter Lead |
| **Guild** | Voluntary, cross-tribe community of practice around a shared interest or skill |
| **Product Owner (PO)** | Sets squad priorities; accountable for squad's business outcomes |
| **Chapter Lead** | Part-time manager of a skill chapter; also a working member of a squad |
| **Tribe Lead** | Servant leader of a tribe; removes obstacles, sets tribe direction |
| **OKR** | Objectives and Key Results — goal-setting framework linking company, tribe, and squad direction |
| **Scrum Master / Agile Coach** | Facilitates squad ceremonies; coaches the team on agile practices |
